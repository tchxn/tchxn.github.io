## The show

*An Extension of Myself* is a collection of prints and mixed-media pieces about the relationship between creativity and identity — each one a different facet of the same person, from vibrant colour to striking poses. It was my first solo exhibition, earned by winning the Tanks Arts Centre Curator's Award at *Energy* 2022, and it opened within a year of finishing school.

## The works

### The Duality of Mankind — as I feed the lion / as I tame the lion

<span class="m dim">2022 · Digital C-type prints</span>

A question about humanity's existence as it spirals into the two-faced reality of the modern era. I drew on my Chinese culture and Christian heritage — traditional Chinese foods, cookware and auspicious guardian lions — and on Greg Semu's *The Last Cannibal Supper*, whose orchestrated clash of cultural food, costume, ritual and biblical staging pushed me towards making "auto-portraits" and using appropriation to challenge my own first ideas.

This is the series that won the Creative Generation Excellence Award and hung at the Gallery of Modern Art in Brisbane.

### The Way of Qian and Kun · Celestial Union

<span class="m dim">2023 · Prints</span>

Three characters from the I Ching — 乾为天 (heaven), 坤为地 (earth) and 乾坤 (the two together) — embodied through pose and a sword that stands for both physical strength and the spiritual realm. *Celestial Union* extends it with Běidǒu (北斗), the seven stars of the Big Dipper, whose double nature links life and death: unity and multiplicity, heaven and earth, yin and yang, held in balance.

### Tones of the Soul: The Multifaceted Self

<span class="m dim">2023 · Print</span>

Four figures, each in a distinct hue — purple, white, green and red — and each holding a sword. The colours are an emotional palette; the swords are how the feeling becomes something you can see.

### Growth through Adversity

<span class="m dim">2023 · Print</span>

Transformation through accepting what's hard rather than avoiding it, rooted in the story of Miyamoto Musashi, the swordsman and philosopher who worked through trial after trial on the way to mastery. At the centre is a sword my grandfather gave me — the idea made physical, and personal.

## What it involved

Beyond making the works: an artist profile, a written description of every piece, a loan agreement for the hanging, the launch talk, working with the gallery on the invitation, and a full exhibition report afterwards.

Showing your own work teaches you something a brief never does — that it only lands if the room, the sequence and the words around it are designed too.

## Why it's in a systems portfolio

Because it's the same question underneath. Everything else here is about two things that are supposed to be separate and aren't: structure and image, the technical and the human, the office and the warehouse. This is where I said that out loud first.
