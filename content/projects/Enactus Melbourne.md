## The situation

Choosing a degree at Melbourne means reading a handbook that tells you which subjects exist, not what the next three years will feel like. Students fill the gap with Reddit threads and whoever they happen to know in the course. Enactus Melbourne, the university's social-enterprise club, set up **GradCap** to close it: real pathways, from real students, before you commit.

The first target was the Faculty of Business and Economics. Teammates interviewed students in each major using a shared question bank: what the course is really like day to day, how hard it is to do well, where people struggle, what they wish they'd known.

## What I designed

**The product in Figma**, from homepage to a testable prototype: quizzes, roadmaps, resources and opportunities, and a personal dashboard.

**A degree map instead of a page of text.** Interview answers are long and uneven, and nobody reads a wall of them. I laid each degree out as a timeline of topics: what it is, day-to-day life, how to do well, common challenges, extracurriculars, misconceptions, outcomes, careers. Selecting a topic opens a card where each answer is broken into short chained "bubbles", so one student's experience reads as a sequence you can follow rather than a quote you have to decode.

## How it was tested

The team ran a feedback week with the prototype sitting next to what students already use, the university handbook and Reddit, so people judged it against the real alternatives, not in isolation. I folded the feedback into the next iteration: the dashboard's "Upcoming deadlines" became **"Your roadmap"**, the current section highlights as you move through a degree map, and the catch-all "Other" category came out of resources.

## What I took from it

Working with people who weren't designers, on a deadline nobody owned, taught me to design for the content we actually had rather than the content we hoped to get. The degree map exists because interview answers came back messy; the structure had to make them readable without rewriting what students said.
