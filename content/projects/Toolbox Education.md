## The situation

Toolbox Education runs programs for schools and for parents. They were expanding interstate with a site that had grown by accretion — more than twenty pages added one at a time, each making sense on its own.

## What I found

The page count wasn't the problem. **Nobody could tell whether a page was written for a parent or for a principal.** Those are different buyers with different questions, and both were landing on the same pages and reading past each other.

Downstream, that meant enquiries arrived without context, landed in the wrong place, and got answered twice or not at all. The interface problem and the operations problem were the same problem.

## What I did

- Restructured the information architecture across **20+ pages**, grouping by who was asking rather than by what the organisation offers
- Separated Victoria and New South Wales cleanly, so the expansion had somewhere to live
- Aligned lead capture and enquiry routing with their **HubSpot pipelines**, so an enquiry arrives tagged with what it's about
- Reduced friction in the call-booking pathway for group bookings

## What I'd note

Most of the value was in surfacing the breakdowns between usability, data capture and internal process, and taking them to the founders as decisions rather than observations.
