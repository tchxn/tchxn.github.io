## The situation

Toolbox Education runs mental-health and wellbeing workshops for primary and secondary schools, plus workshops for parents and staff. They were expanding interstate with a site that had grown by accretion — more than twenty pages added one at a time, each making sense on its own.

## What I found

I started with an audit of every page, logged as I went. The individual problems were small: the "School programs" menu item didn't lead anywhere until you picked a sub-category; hover states didn't show; one "email us directly" link didn't work; one program page offered **three different ways to email**. On the parent and staff page, the booking button sat below all the detail — by the time you'd read enough to want it, it was gone.

Underneath those was one bigger one. **Nobody could tell whether a page was written for a parent or for a principal.** Those are different buyers with different questions, and both were landing on the same pages and reading past each other. Downstream, that meant enquiries arrived without context, landed in the wrong place, and got answered twice or not at all. The interface problem and the operations problem were the same problem.

## What I did

**Restructured the site around who's asking.** Programs split cleanly into Primary and Secondary, each with its own overview page and a page per workshop, and Victoria and New South Wales separated so the expansion had somewhere to live. The navigation was rebuilt to match, made responsive, and given one clear call to action in the header. Parent and staff workshops moved the booking to the top of the page.

**Built the program pages inside the platform's limits.** The site runs on Squarespace, which has no accordion and little layout control. Rather than fight it or rebuild the site, I wrote custom HTML and CSS code blocks so each overview reads at a glance — one row per workshop, name and a one-line outcome — mirroring the printed program boards schools already recognised.

**Wired enquiries into HubSpot.** Lead capture and call booking were aligned with their **HubSpot pipelines**, so an enquiry arrives tagged with what it's about. I brought the booking calendar forward so it opens straight from the page rather than from a separate contact flow — seeing the times straight away is what gets someone to book. The contact page was reordered so email and phone come first, and its vague "Role" field became "Enquiry".

**Fixed what schools thought they were buying.** Schools were reading each workshop as a single standalone session. It isn't — each is a core workshop plus follow-up activities for the class. I redesigned the workshop slides in the pitch deck around a consistent "what's included" panel — core workshop, small-group reflection, teacher-led follow-up — so it reads as a program, not a one-off.

**Redesigned the brochure.** Primary and Secondary were blurring into one eleven-item list. A calmer palette with a distinct colour for each, matched across both pages, made them read as two sets at a glance. A QR code links print back to the site.

## Beyond the screen

Toolbox is small, so the role stretched. I worked the booth at an education summit and cut a short reel for it, filmed and photographed an in-school workshop for marketing, shot headshots for the team, updated both brand logos on the site, and added downloadable program overviews for schools to take to their leadership.

## What I'd note

Most of the value was in surfacing the breakdowns between usability, data capture and internal process, and taking them to the founders as decisions rather than observations. The best fixes were often the smallest — one booking button in the right place — once the real question was clear.
